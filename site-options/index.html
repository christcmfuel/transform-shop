Recommendation

If the goal is more free-session bookings, pick neighbours. It scored highest on the booking path (8) and the voice (8), every booking control on it says the same thing ('Book a free session') next to a reassurance line, the three-step 'What to expect when you walk in' block answers the beginner's real questions (where, who, what happens), and the mobile sticky bar is there from first paint. Its three fixes are small and mostly deletions: cut the spinning badge and the pulsing button, put 'From £39.99 a month' in the hero facts, and give the chips a 44px tap target. Do those before it goes live; without the price fix a phone user scrolls past coaches, a timetable, nine photos and HYROX before seeing a pound sign. Sessions is the honest runner-up for the same goal: it is the clearest page on a phone and the timetable-as-hero is a genuinely good idea for a gym whose pitch is coached classes every day, but as built it loses the gym's voice entirely and its phone plan cards are a dead end, so it needs the join buttons and the two signature lines put back before it would out-book neighbours. If the goal is standing out next to other Plymouth gyms, pick damn. Nothing else in the city looks like it, the first-week itinerary is the single best new component across all four options, and its photos are treated with more care than anywhere else. But fix the Contents strip on phones first, and accept that the voice judge and the craft judge will never agree on its magazine dressing: keep the serif and the captions, lose the drop caps and 'Plan one/two/three'. A hybrid is worth it in one narrow form only: take neighbours as the base (its structure, copy, named coaches and plan helper) and drop sessions' colour-coded, today-highlighted timetable in place of neighbours' filterable card grid, with the price added to the hero. Both are single-file pages, both use Archivo for body text, and both mark up the classes as day cards with data-type attributes, so this is a day or two of careful work, not a rebuild. Do not try to fold damn's serif editorial system into either of them; it is a different idea and the result would be neither. Floor should not be picked: it is the live page with the volume turned up, and it averaged 5.4 against a baseline of 5.

Pairing with the kit store

damn pairs with Chalk (light serif): same cream paper, hairline rules and serif headlines, so the shop reads as another page of the same programme. floor pairs with Blackout (dark video): the dark ground, neon green and stencil type carry straight across. neighbours pairs with People (warm rounded): cream, rounded white cards and the Cosmo thread belong together. sessions pairs with Field (olive utility): the mono labels, 1.5px ink borders and noticeboard register are closest to a utility store; if Field's olive clashes with the six class colours on the timetable, Chalk is the fallback because it shares the cream ground and restraint.

What only you can supply

To lift every option

  • Coach photos and one line each (Narcisse, Eliya, Kirsty, Brad, Ava): role, what they coach, one human fact. All four options show five bare names labelled 'Coach'. Ask which coach takes Foundations on Wednesdays and name them in the Foundations block and the hero, so a beginner knows who they'll meet.
  • Actual HIIT times (Mon/Wed/Fri) and the HYROX session slots. Every option prints 'HIIT · Evening' with no time, and HYROX is 'included' on plans but absent from the timetable. Also the true weekly class count: the timetable on every page lists 15 sessions while the stat strip says '20+'.
  • A 25-word description for each class (Circuits, Strength, HIIT, Yoga, Pilates, Foundations, HYROX): what it is, how long it runs, roughly how many people, who it suits, what to bring. No option explains any class; all assume the names are self-explanatory to a beginner.
  • What the free session actually is: length, format (tour plus a class, or a one-to-one), what to wear and bring, and which hours it can be booked. Used for a 'what happens at your free session' block and FAQ on whichever option wins; today it is a Calendly link and a slogan.
  • Staffed / front-desk hours and the 24/7 entry method (fob, app, PIN). All pages present '24/7' as if a first-timer can walk in at 3am and meet someone. Used in the info strip, FAQ and the 'collect kit from the front desk' line.
  • The members app name plus App Store and Google Play links. 'Members app' is listed as a plan feature on every option with no name and no link. If it is the PushPress member app, say so and link it.
  • Real Google review data: the live review URL (the g.page/r/transform-fitness-plympton/review link on every page looks like a placeholder), the review count to sit beside '5 stars', and 4 to 6 verbatim reviews with names as shown on Google and dates. Confirm or drop 'Member since 2024' on Sarah, Mark and Laura; the repo docs say the gym opened Nov 2025. Provide evidence for 'best reviewed in Plymouth' and 'fastest growing', or soften both.
  • Member photos with written consent, specifically: two or three beginners (including women and over-50s), Foundations actually running (sessions uses a promo graphic), a race-day team shot, plus exterior, entrance and car-park photos and a night shot to prove 24/7. All four options recycle the same twelve images and none shows the building on Langage Industrial Estate.
  • HYROX specifics: the next two race dates and cities the club is training for, any team name, whether beginners can join HYROX sessions, and whether Hybrid covers them. Options contradict each other: damn says HYROX is covered by Hybrid's sessions; neighbours and sessions say it is Unlimited only.
  • An 80 to 120 word founder story from Mike with a photo: why he opened it, when (Nov 2025), why Plympton. No option names the owner even though the free-session Calendly is his. Used as a short 'from the owner' block near the reviews.
  • Plan fine print: yearly plan prices, any joining fee, notice period behind 'cancel anytime', minimum age, student / NHS / forces / couples discounts (Plymouth is a forces city), PT prices or packages, and how a day pass is bought. 'No hidden fees' appears on every page with none of this stated.
  • Facilities list for the FAQ: changing rooms, showers, lockers, toilets, step-free access, water. Nothing on any option answers the questions a nervous beginner asks before a first visit.
  • Brand decision: three straplines are competing for the hero ('gives a damn', 'No egos. No judgement.', 'Everyone knows your name here'). Pick the one to lead with and which become supporting lines, so the winning option can be tightened rather than carrying all three.

Still weak on all four

Known gaps

  • Coaches are names only on all four: no faces, no roles, no specialisms, no 'who takes Foundations'. For a beginner deciding whether to book, the people are the product and none of the pages shows them.
  • HIIT has no time anywhere ('Evening'), HYROX sessions are not on the timetable despite being sold as a plan feature, and every page's '20+ classes a week' stat sits above a timetable showing 15. Floor even labels its HIIT band 'illustrative'.
  • No option describes a single class. A first-timer cannot tell Circuits from HIIT from Strength, how long a session is, how many people are in it, or what to bring.
  • The free session is a Calendly link plus 'walk in, train hard'. None says how long it is, whether it is a class or a tour, what to wear, who you'll meet, or when it can happen. Sessions' four-step strip is the closest and still says nothing concrete.
  • '24/7' is everywhere; staffed hours are nowhere. A beginner is told the door is always open but not when a human is behind the desk to collect kit, take a day pass or run the free session.
  • Personal training gets no homepage presence beyond a nav link on all four, despite being one of three revenue lines and 'one-to-one PT' appearing in the expert-coaching copy.
  • Testimonials are lifted unchanged from the baseline: initials for avatars, no photos, 'Member since 2024' on three quotes for a gym that opened Nov 2025, no review count beside '5 stars', and 'best reviewed in Plymouth' and 'fastest growing' with nothing behind them. The Google review URL looks invented.
  • Plan facts disagree across options on whether Hybrid includes HYROX, yoga and Pilates. Whichever wins, the plan card, the FAQ and the HYROX block need to say the same thing, and only Chris can settle which is true.
